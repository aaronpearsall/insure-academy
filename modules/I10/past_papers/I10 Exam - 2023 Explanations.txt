I10 INSURANCE BROKING FUNDAMENTALS - I10 Exam - 2023
================================================================================
Explanations for questions from this past paper. I10 Insurance Broking Fundamentals.
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 1
Where a retail broker uses a wholesale broker to place business, who has a duty of care to the insured?

A. It is shared equally between the brokers.
B. It remains with the retail broker.
C. It solely depends on the terms of business agreed between the brokers.
D. It transfers from the retail broker to the wholesale broker.

Answer: B
Explanation: The retail broker holds the agency relationship with the insured and remains responsible for advising and placing cover appropriately. Using a wholesale broker does not transfer the retail broker's duty of care to the client.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 2
How much commission does an insurance broker receive on placement of an insurance policy with a premium of £500 plus Insurance Premium Tax and a 20% commission rate?

A. £88.
B. £100.
C. £112.
D. £120.

Answer: B
Explanation: Broker commission is calculated on the insurance premium before Insurance Premium Tax. Twenty per cent of £500 is £100; IPT is collected separately and is not part of the commission base.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 3
One of the key roles of an insurance broker is to

A. act as an intermediary between the insurer and the regulator.
B. calculate the insurance premium.
C. conduct insurance mediation on behalf of insurers.
D. provide independent, impartial advice to clients.

Answer: D
Explanation: A core broking duty is to give independent, impartial advice so clients can make informed decisions. Brokers do not act for regulators, set insurer premiums as their main role, or conduct mediation solely on behalf of insurers.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 4
How does the use of an insurance broker benefit an insurer?

A. All material circumstances will always be declared by the broker to the insurer.
B. It enables the insurer to easily cross-sell insurance policies for other classes of business to an existing insured.
C. It reduces the time spent by the insurer in explaining and administering a policyholder's insurance requirements.
D. The responsibility for the payment of Insurance Premium Tax is passed to the broker.

Answer: C
Explanation: Brokers gather and present risk information and handle much of the administration of placing and servicing business, which reduces the time insurers spend explaining requirements to policyholders.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 5
What type(s) of insurance broker is able to offer Lloyd's policies to its clients?

A. Aggregators only.
B. Lloyd's brokers only.
C. Wholesale brokers only.
D. All types of insurance broker.

Answer: D
Explanation: Any type of insurance broker may arrange Lloyd's business, typically by using a Lloyd's broker or appropriate market access; access is not restricted to aggregators or wholesale brokers alone.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 6
The back office function within a firm of insurance brokers will NOT include

A. compliance.
B. marketing.
C. placement of risks.
D. settlement of insurer accounts.

Answer: C
Explanation: Placement of risks is a front-office, client-facing production activity. Back office functions include compliance, marketing support, and settling insurer accounts.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 7
Insurance brokers are least likely to be involved in placing which type of insurance?

A. Construction.
B. Extended warranty.
C. Marine cargo.
D. Motor fleet.

Answer: B
Explanation: Extended warranty is commonly sold at point of sale through retailers or specialist schemes rather than through traditional insurance brokers placing commercial or specialty risks.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 8
Under a contract of insurance, what is brokerage?

A. The commission paid to the broker by the insurer, which is included in the premium.
B. The commission rebated by the insurer to the insured in cases with limited broker involvement.
C. A fee paid by the insured directly to the broker.
D. A fee paid by the insured directly to the insurer, in addition to the premium.

Answer: A
Explanation: Brokerage is the remuneration paid by the insurer to the broker, usually as commission included within the premium paid by the insured.

Question 9
When a Lloyd's broker agrees to place business on behalf of a broker who is NOT a Lloyd's broker, the Lloyd's broker is operating as a

A. consolidator.
B. producing broker.
C. retail broker.
D. wholesale broker.

Answer: D
Explanation: When a Lloyd's broker places business on behalf of a non-Lloyd's broker, it acts as a wholesale broker relative to the producing retail broker.

Question 10
What is the role of reinsurance brokers?

A. Aggregators.

C. Market placement specialists.
D. Risk surveyors.

Answer: C
Explanation: Reinsurance brokers specialise in placing reinsurance in the market, acting as expert intermediaries between ceding insurers and reinsurers.

Question 11

A. By advising the insurer's clients on risk reduction.
B. By advising the insurer regarding warranties and exclusions.

Question 14
Under a delegated underwriting authority agreement, when would a coverholder normally declare bound risks by completing a bordereau and submitting it to the insurer?

A. Immediately the risk is bound.
B. Weekly.
C. Monthly.

Answer: C

Question 15
If a proposer requests that an excess does NOT apply to his new insurance policy, the insurance broker should explain the

A. consequent limitations on cover.
B. effect on premium levels.
C. imposition of warranties.
D. restrictions on the policy term.

Answer: B
Explanation: Removing an excess generally increases the insurer's exposure to frequent smaller losses, so the broker should explain that premiums are likely to rise.

Question 20
The Insurance Act 2015 emphasises what role for brokers with regard to material circumstances?

A. To ensure all material circumstances are supplied to enable accurate presentation of the risk.

C. To explain to the proposer that failure to disclose a material circumstance is an offence under the Act.

Explanation: The Insurance Act 2015 focuses on fair presentation of the risk; brokers should help ensure all material circumstances are disclosed to insurers.

Question 21
What is one of the benefits to the client where an insurance broker provides a detailed underwriting submission to an insurer?

A. A faster quotation will always be obtained.
B. Fewer exclusions and warranties.
C. Increased probability of more favourable terms.
D. Wider cover will always be offered.

Answer: C
Explanation: A thorough underwriting submission helps insurers understand the risk, which can improve terms, pricing, and capacity rather than guaranteeing wider cover.

B. That the insurer is regulated to underwrite the required class of insurance.

Question 23
Where a broker operates a no service claims-handling arrangement, what action must be taken when a claim is notified by a client?

A. Advise the client that the broker can only become involved if there are problems with the claim.
B. Advise the client that they have a duty to notify the insurer and the broker has no involvement.
C. Advise the insurer that a claim is being made but also tell the client to deal directly with the insurer.
D. Advise the insurer that the broker will forward the initial paperwork but that the insurer must then respond directly to the client.

Answer: C
Explanation: Under a no-service claims arrangement, the broker notifies the insurer but the client deals directly with the insurer for claim handling.

Question 24
Whom should an insurance broker inform if he suspects that a claim may be fraudulent?

A. The claimant.
B. The insurer.
C. The police.
D. The regulator.

Answer: B
Explanation: Suspected fraudulent claims should be reported to the insurer, which decides investigation and any report to authorities; the broker should not tip off the claimant.

Question 25
An insurance broker suggests to a client that risk control measures are introduced to limit the extent of any physical damage if a loss occurs. This is known as

A. risk avoidance.
B. risk elimination.
C. risk minimisation.
D. risk prevention.

Answer: C
Explanation: Measures that limit the severity of damage if a loss occurs are risk minimisation (or reduction), distinct from prevention or avoidance.

Question 26
An insurance broker's risk survey has identified the potential for small, high-frequency claims. The same risk also results in occasional significantly higher claim values. What is likely to be the most appropriate recommendation from the broker based on this information?

A. Advise the client to self-insure in respect of this entire risk.
B. Exclude this risk from cover.
C. Make this risk subject to a warranty.
D. Set an excess to eliminate the smaller claims.

Answer: D
Explanation: An excess appropriate to the frequent small losses can improve the cost of cover while leaving protection for larger losses that remain significant.
