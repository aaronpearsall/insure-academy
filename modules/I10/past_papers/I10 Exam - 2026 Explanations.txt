I10 INSURANCE BROKING FUNDAMENTALS - I10 Exam - 2026
================================================================================
Explanations for questions from this past paper. I10 Insurance Broking Fundamentals.
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 1
One of the main differences between a wholesale broker and a retail broker, involved in the same placement, is that only the retail broker

A. acts on behalf of the principal.
B. deals directly with the proposer.
C. is remunerated from the overall commission available.
D. will have access to the Lloyd's market.

Answer: B
Explanation: The retail broker has the direct client relationship and deals with the proposer. The wholesale broker is instructed by the retail broker and places business in specialist markets such as Lloyd's.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 2
The principal of a reinsurance broker is the

A. insurance broker.
B. insured.
C. insurer.
D. reinsurer.

Answer: C
Explanation: A reinsurance broker acts for the ceding insurer as principal when placing reinsurance. The insured is the principal of a direct insurance broker, not of a reinsurance broker.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 3
What is the benefit to an insurer of having an insurance policy placed through an insurance broker?

A. Full disclosure of the policyholder's material circumstances will always be made.
B. The insurance broker is responsible for paying the insurance premium in the event of non-payment by the policyholder.
C. The insurance broker will explain the technical aspects of the insurance policy to the policyholder.
D. The policyholder retention rate is always greater than that achieved for a direct placement.

Answer: C
Explanation: Brokers add value for insurers by helping policyholders understand technical policy terms, improving communication and reducing misunderstandings that can lead to disputes.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 4
What type of risks are placed by insurance brokers?

A. All types of risk.
B. Commercial risks only.
C. Global risks only.
D. Personal lines risk only.

Answer: A
Explanation: Insurance brokers may place personal lines, commercial, London Market and specialist risks. They are not restricted to one category of business.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 5
Within an insurance brokerage, who is usually responsible for ensuring that mediation activities comply with the Financial Conduct Authority's requirements?

A. An account executive.
B. The compliance officer.
C. The data protection officer.
D. The finance director.

Answer: B
Explanation: The compliance officer (or compliance function) is responsible for ensuring the firm's mediation activities meet FCA rules, including conduct, disclosure and systems and controls.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 6
When a Lloyd's insurance broker uses the subscription market to place an insurance policy, this is a process of

A. co-insurance.
B. co-ordination.
C. consolidation.
D. contribution.

Answer: A
Explanation: Placing a risk with several Lloyd's underwriters on a subscription basis is co-insurance: each underwriter takes a stated proportion of the same risk under common terms.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 7
A firm of insurance brokers which establishes a network will seek to attract members who are

A. clients.
B. insurers.
C. other insurance brokers.
D. reinsurers.

Answer: C
Explanation: A broker network is a group of independent brokers sharing services, market access or branding. The network head seeks to attract other insurance brokers as members.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 8
An insurance broker obtains quotations from a number of insurers for a client. In what circumstances may the broker take into account the amount of commission that will be paid when deciding which insurer to recommend?

A. Where no fee is charged in addition to the commission earned.
B. Where the approval of the regulator has been obtained.
C. Where the client's interests are not prejudiced.
D. Where the policy is placed via Lloyd's.

Answer: C
Explanation: Commission must not influence the recommendation if it prejudices the client's interests. Where products are equally suitable, commission may be considered only if the client's interests are not prejudiced.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 9
Why does an insurer use a reinsurance broker to place its property catastrophe worldwide reinsurance exposure?

A. To provide an enhanced claims service.
B. To reduce its administration expenses.
C. To use the reinsurance broker's expertise to reinsure the exposure with one large reinsurer only.
D. To use the reinsurance broker's expertise to reinsure the exposure with a number of financially strong reinsurers.

Answer: D
Explanation: Property catastrophe reinsurance is typically spread across several financially strong reinsurers. The reinsurance broker's market knowledge and relationships help achieve effective placement and diversification.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 10
Who instructs a wholesale insurance broker to place an insurance policy?

A. An appointed representative.
B. An insured.
C. An insurer.
D. A retail insurance broker.

Answer: D
Explanation: A wholesale broker is usually instructed by a retail insurance broker (or sometimes an insurer), not directly by the insured in a two-tier placement.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 11
Which element of the amount payable by a client must an insurance broker show separately on an invoice?

A. Commission.
B. Fee.
C. Administrative charges.
D. Work transfer fee.

Answer: B
Explanation: Where a broker charges a separate fee, it must be shown distinctly on the invoice so the client can see remuneration beyond any commission included in the premium.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 12
How does an insurance broker who has a claims advocacy role assist and advise the client?

A. It handles all aspects of the claim.
B. It assists the client when problems arise which cannot be resolved directly with the insurer.
C. When requested to by the insurer to expedite the claim process.
D. When specialist or complex risks are involved.

Answer: B
Explanation: Claims advocacy means supporting the client when issues arise that cannot be resolved directly with the insurer, rather than handling every aspect of the claim regardless of difficulty.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 13
What should be a major consideration for an insurance broker when designing a global insurance programme for a multinational client?

A. Achieving the lowest possible insurance premium.
B. Avoiding gaps in insurance coverage.
C. Ensuring that the client discloses all material information.
D. Obtaining identical insurance coverage for each territory.

Answer: B
Explanation: For multinational programmes, a primary aim is continuity of cover across territories and avoiding gaps or overlaps between local policies and a master programme.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 14
What action should an insurance broker immediately take upon realisation that it will be unable to fully meet a client's demands and needs?

A. Inform the client of policy restrictions in coverage.
B. Insert any policy restrictions into the insurance policy.
C. Send the client a revised demands and needs statement.
D. Terminate the insurance placement.

Answer: A
Explanation: If the broker cannot fully meet the client's demands and needs, it must promptly inform the client of relevant restrictions or limitations in the cover being arranged.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 15
A coverholder under a delegated authority agreement will be an employee of

A. an insurance broker.
B. an insurer.
C. the policyholder.
D. the reinsurer.

Answer: A
Explanation: Under delegated authority, the coverholder is normally an employee of the insurance broker (or insurer) authorised to underwrite and bind within agreed limits.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 16
If a proposer requests that an excess does NOT apply to his new insurance policy, the insurance broker should explain the

A. consequent limitations on cover.
B. effect on premium levels.
C. imposition of warranties.
D. restrictions on the policy term.

Answer: B
Explanation: Removing or reducing an excess usually increases the premium because the insurer bears a greater proportion of each loss. The broker should explain this trade-off.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 17
Which document does a Lloyd's insurance broker use to place business with Lloyd's underwriters?

A. A bordereau.
B. A certificate of insurance.
C. A Market Reform Contract.
D. A Terms of Business Agreement.

Answer: C
Explanation: Lloyd's brokers place business with Lloyd's underwriters using a Market Reform Contract (MRC), the standard London Market placement document.

Question 18
What characteristics does a Lloyd's insurance broker look for in an insurer when selecting a leading underwriter to provide a quotation on a risk in the subscription market?

A. The ability to always underwrite the largest proportion of the insurance risk.
B. The ability to provide the lowest premium quotation which is then supported by following insurers subject to different terms and conditions.
C. The capacity to underwrite 100% of the risk which is then automatically reinsured to following insurers.
D. Specialised knowledge of the class of business to provide a quotation which will be supported by following insurers.

Answer: D
Explanation: The leading underwriter must have recognised expertise in the class so that following insurers will support the leader's terms, premium and conditions in the subscription market.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 19
A proposal form is being completed in respect of a professional indemnity risk. What is the broker's usual role in this process?

A. To advise the client on what material circumstances to disclose on the form.
B. To complete the form and ask the client to verify its accuracy before signing it.
C. To complete those questions where the client is unsure of the meaning.
D. To verify the accuracy of the information provided by the client before asking the client to sign it.

Answer: A
Explanation: The broker's usual role is to advise the client what material circumstances must be disclosed; the client completes and signs the proposal, retaining responsibility for the answers.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 20
Why might an insurance broker recommend a packaged insurance policy to a client?

A. To cover a number of risks under one policy.
B. Long-term insurance coverage is guaranteed.
C. A lower excess is always available.
D. To provide an individually-designed insurance policy.

Answer: A
Explanation: Packaged policies combine several covers under one policy, which can simplify administration and may be cost-effective for standard commercial needs.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 21
An insurance broker has regularly placed a certain type of risk with a leading insurer, but market trading conditions have changed. How should the broker approach the renewal?

A. Maintain continuity with this insurer.
B. Negotiate improvements in terms and conditions with this insurer.
C. Review breadth of cover across the market sector.
D. Review capacity across the market sector leaders.

Answer: C
Explanation: When trading conditions change, the broker should review breadth of cover across the market sector rather than assuming continuity with the incumbent insurer is still best for the client.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 22
What factor must an insurance broker take into account when deciding which insurers to approach for a quotation?

A. The amount of commission paid by the insurer.
B. The financial security of the insurer.
C. Whether a delegated authority agreement is in place with the insurer.
D. Whether the insurer offers monthly instalments for payment of the premium.

Answer: B
Explanation: Financial security of insurers is essential so that claims can be met. Brokers must consider security ratings and capacity alongside price and coverage.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 23
A recognised advantage to a client of using an insurance broker's risk survey service is that it can

A. guarantee access to insurance at a competitive premium.
B. identify risks of which the client may otherwise be unaware.
C. provide certainty of a cost-effective management solution.
D. simplify the process of obtaining cover for otherwise uninsurable risks.

Answer: B
Explanation: Risk surveys help identify hazards and exposures the client may not have recognised, supporting better risk management and more appropriate insurance.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 24
An insurance broker is reviewing an insurance programme for a client at a time of increasing competition for restricted capacity in the market. What should the broker recommend in order to maintain existing premium levels?

A. Improvements to risk management.
B. Payment of the premium in instalments.
C. A reduction in sums insured.
D. The removal of warranties and exclusions.

Answer: A
Explanation: In a hard market with restricted capacity, stronger risk management can help justify existing premium levels to underwriters seeking quality risks.

Question 25
An insurance broker underwrites risks on a full delegated authority basis. What duties does the broker perform?

A. Accept the business and calculate the premium only.
B. Accept the business and issue the documentation only.
C. Calculate the premium and issue the documentation only.
D. Accept the business, calculate the premium and issue the documentation.

Answer: D
Explanation: Full delegated authority requires the broker to accept risks within limits, calculate premium and issue documentation on the insurer's behalf.

Question 26
Which party under a delegated authority agreement will normally be responsible for underwriting risks?

A. The coverholder.
B. The insurer.
C. The reinsurer.
D. The retail broker.

Answer: A
Explanation: The coverholder (broker or other delegate) normally underwrites and binds business under the delegated authority agreement, within prescribed limits.

Question 27
What can a broker incorporate into an insurance programme for a client who wishes to maintain rates and terms for a set period?

A. Higher excesses.
B. A long-term agreement.
C. A packaged policy with a single insurer.
D. A risk management review.

Answer: B
Explanation: A long-term agreement (LTA) can fix rates and terms for an agreed period, giving the client premium and coverage stability subject to market practice.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 28
What must an insurance broker include within a policyholder's suitability statement?

A. A financial report on the insurer that is being recommended to the policyholder.

Answer: C
Explanation: The suitability statement must explain why the recommended policy is suitable and how it meets the policyholder's demands and needs—not merely list quotations.

Question 30
When incorrect documents are received from an insurer, in what circumstances, if any, can the insurance broker send them to the client to enable it to meet contract certainty timelines?

A. In all circumstances.
B. In no circumstances.

A. No service.
B. Claims advocacy.

Question 32
When providing claims management support, to which party does the broker's main responsibility lie?

A. The adjuster.
B. The insured.
C. The insurer.
D. The regulator.

Answer: B
Explanation: When providing claims management support, the broker's primary duty is to the insured client, even though it must also deal fairly with insurers.

Question 33
Which document is used to detail each party's duties in the insurance placement process when a retail insurance broker and a wholesale insurance broker are both involved in placing an insurance policy?

A. A demands and needs statement.
B. An initial disclosure document.

Explanation: A Terms of Business Agreement between retail and wholesale brokers sets out each party's duties, authority and responsibilities in the placement chain.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 34
An insurance broker has two clients competing for limited market capacity. In order to manage a potential conflict of interest, what should the broker do?

A. Agree to receive a reduced fee or commission in respect of the placement of this business.
B. Disclose the details to both clients and obtain their agreement on how to proceed.
C. Disclose the details to potential insurers in order to negotiate increased capacity.

Answer: B
Explanation: Where two clients compete for limited capacity, the broker should disclose the conflict to both clients and agree how to proceed, managing the conflict transparently.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 35
What type of task may an insurance broker outsource without seeking the client's permission?

A. An additional service for which the client has paid a fee.
B. A service which requires a level of expertise outside the broker's skill set.
C. One which requires no question of judgement or advice.
D. One which requires the safekeeping of client monies.

Answer: C
Explanation: Routine administrative tasks requiring no judgement or advice may be outsourced without specific client consent; advice, judgement and client money require greater controls.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 36
Which party(ies) may be the principal of an insurance broker when the broker is dealing with a claim?

A. The insurer only.
B. The policyholder only.
C. The third party only.
D. Both the insurer and the policyholder.

Answer: D
Explanation: On claims, the broker may act for the policyholder (insured) or, under delegated claims authority, for the insurer—either can be principal depending on the arrangement.

Question 37
What will be included in an insurance broker's Terms of Business Agreement with a client concerning the duty of disclosure?

Explanation: The TOBA should remind the client of the duty of fair presentation/disclosure and the serious consequences of failing to disclose material circumstances.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 38
What will be warranted within the regulatory status section of a Terms of Business Agreement between an insurer and an insurance broker?

Question 39
A firm of motor insurance brokers receives notification of a motor claim in which two separate clients are involved, each insured with a different insurer. What should the claims handler consider?

Question 40

Question 41

A. believed to pose the greatest risk to commercial clients.

Question 42

A. acceptable provided it is a Lloyd's broker.

Question 43
Which body is responsible for the regulation of Lloyd's brokers?

A. British Insurance Brokers' Association.
B. Council of Lloyd's.
C. Financial Conduct Authority.
D. Prudential Regulation Authority.

Explanation: Lloyd's brokers are regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (with Lloyd's also exercising market governance through the Council of Lloyd's).

Question 44
A commercial client renews its property owner's insurance policy and cancels the cover within 14 days as it has found a cheaper alternative. What premium refund, if any, is it most likely to receive?

A. A full refund in all circumstances.
B. A full refund, providing no claims have been notified.
C. A pro-rata refund, providing no claims have been notified.
D. No refund will be received.

Answer: C
Explanation: Commercial customers do not have the same cooling-off rights as consumers; cancellation within 14 days typically attracts a pro-rata refund if no claims have been notified.

Question 45
In which document does the Financial Conduct Authority require that an insurance broker discloses its address and authorisation status?

A. demands and needs statement.
B. initial disclosure document.
C. insurance policy.
D. statement of facts.

Explanation: The initial disclosure document (or equivalent disclosure under IDD/COBS) must include the broker's address and FCA authorisation status before business is conducted.

Question 46
One of the requirements that the Financial Conduct Authority places on a firm of insurance brokers to ensure competency of employees dealing with consumers, is that they must

Question 47

With what minimum frequency must earned commission be taken from insurer's funds?

B. Client assets.
